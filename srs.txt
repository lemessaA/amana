 1. Introduction and scope
 1.1 Purpose
 AMANA helps people make more informed decisions about second-hand phones and computers by recording device
 identifiers, user claims, supporting evidence, theft allegations, investigation decisions, restrictions, and AMANA-approved
 transfer history. It provides a limited public status lookup and controlled authenticated workflows.

 1.2 Product objectives
 • Allow a claimant to register a phone or computer using a supported identifier.

 • Accept optional purchase-receipt evidence and permit later submission against the same claim.

 • Distinguish a theft allegation, an active investigation, and a substantiated finding.

 • Prevent eligible transfers from bypassing current restrictions, competing claims, or recipient consent.

 • Retain proportionate audit history for dispute and abuse review while protecting personal information.

 • Give public lookup users a cautious status result without exposing claimant or reporter identities.

 1.3 In scope
 Account registration, authentication, password recovery and deactivation; device and identifier registration; receipt submission
 and review; theft reporting and investigation; claim disputes; identifier-conflict review; association release; AMANA-approved
 transfer; public lookup; audit history; notifications; rate limiting; English and Amharic UI; and security tests.

 1.4 Out of scope
 GPS tracking, remote locking, device recovery, payments, a marketplace, blockchain, AI-based fraud detection, automated
 vulnerability scanning, and integrations with police, Fayda, telecom operators, manufacturers, or government systems. AMANA
 does not guarantee legal ownership, genuine hardware identity, or that a device has never been stolen.

 1.5 Standards note
 The document follows requirements-engineering practices associated with ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148. It does not claim formal
 certification or legal compliance. Applicable privacy and legal obligations must be reviewed before production deployment.

 2. Definitions and state model
   Term                                    Normative definition

   Device                                  A physical phone or computer represented by one internal AMANA device record.

   Device identifier                       An IMEI or manufacturer-assigned computer serial number associated with a device record.

   Claim                                   A user's recorded assertion of association with a device; it is not by itself proof of legal ownership.

   Claimant                                The user linked to a device through an active claim/association.

   Affected claimant                       The claimant whose association or permissions are directly affected by a case. A reporter is not
                                           automatically the affected claimant.

   Receipt status                          One of receipt-free, receipt-pending, receipt-accepted, or receipt-rejected.

   Acquisition basis                       The reason the claim was created, stored separately from receipt status: accepted receipt-backed
                                           claim, completed AMANA-approved transfer, or other/unverified basis.

   Theft report                            An allegation that a device may be stolen.

   Investigation                           A formal case created after a report passes screening.

   Provisional restriction                 A temporary restriction applied when a report passes screening, subject to reviewer triage within 72
                                           hours.

   Active theft restriction                A restriction supported by a documented substantiated finding.

   Inconclusive restriction                A restriction retained after an inconclusive finding with a recorded reason and review date.

   Claim-dispute case                      A process to assess competing claims without creating a second active association.

   Identifier-conflict case                A review of a newly submitted identifier that conflicts with an existing record; the new submission is
                                           held, not the existing claimant by default.

   Association release                     Ending a claimant's active association. It does not prove sale, transfer legal title, delete the device,
                                           or erase history.

   AMANA-approved transfer                 A transfer completed through the authenticated workflow after eligibility checks and recipient
                                           acceptance.

   Account deactivation                    Disabling ordinary login/use while retaining records required for active cases, security, audit, and
                                           approved retention rules.

   Reviewer / senior reviewer              Authorized case decision-maker / reviewer permitted to approve extensions and independent
                                           reconsiderations.

   System administrator                    Operator of technical configuration and access. Administrative access alone does not authorize case
                                           decisions.


 2.1 Separate state dimensions
 The system shall not collapse these independent dimensions into one status: (1) receipt evidence status, (2) acquisition basis,
 (3) claim status, (4) association status, (5) report/investigation status, (6) restriction status, (7) transfer status, and (8) account
 status.

 2.2 Effective status
 The device's effective public status is derived at request time from current restrictions and cases using the precedence in
 Section 5. It is not independently editable. Historical reports alone do not create a current restriction.

 2.3 Normative wording
 “Shall” denotes a mandatory requirement. “May” denotes permitted behavior. “Should” denotes a recommendation that
 requires a documented exception if not followed.


 3. Core use cases and scenarios
 UC-01: Register a device
 A registered user submits an IMEI or computer serial number and creates a claim. Receipt evidence is optional. The system
 validates syntax and uniqueness, checks current restrictions, records the acquisition basis and receipt status separately, and
 does not represent registration as proof of ownership.

 UC-02: Submit and review receipt evidence
 A claimant uploads a supported receipt file during registration or later. Evidence remains pending until an authorized reviewer
 accepts, rejects, or requests correction with a recorded reason. Acceptance does not remove unrelated restrictions or
 authorize past transfers.

 UC-03: Report possible theft
 An authenticated user submits a report. Screening checks required fields, identifier syntax, duplicate patterns and rate limits. A
 passing report atomically creates or links an investigation and applies a provisional restriction. A reviewer triages within 72
 hours.

 UC-04: Resolve competing claims
 A second claimant cannot create a second active association. The request is held and routed to a claim-dispute case. The
 current claimant may voluntarily release their association, but release does not clear independent restrictions or prove the new
 claimant's ownership.

 UC-05: Transfer a device
 An eligible claimant initiates a transfer to a recipient. The recipient must explicitly accept within 72 hours. The server rechecks
 eligibility and restrictions immediately before transactional completion. Decline, cancellation or expiry leaves the source
 association unchanged.

 UC-06: Look up a device
 A user enters a supported identifier and receives only a generic effective status and explanation. The result must not reveal
 whether a named person registered the device, who reported it, or what evidence reviewers saw.

 Scenario 1: Receipt accepted, no current blocker
 The claim may initiate an AMANA-approved transfer if its acquisition basis is eligible, the association is active, no restriction or
 dispute blocks the operation, and the recipient accepts. Association release is also allowed when no hold applies.

 Scenario 2: No accepted receipt, no current blocker
 A claim with no accepted receipt and no completed AMANA-transfer basis cannot initiate an AMANA-approved transfer. The
 claimant may release the association if no blocking condition applies. Release is not a verified sale or transfer. A later claimant
 must create a new claim and cannot inherit transfer eligibility merely because the prior claimant released.

 Scenario 3: Theft report and receipt-backed claim
 A passing report creates or joins the active investigation and applies a provisional restriction. Triage is due within 72 hours.
 Investigation deadline: 720 hours from opening, UTC. One extension of up to 360 hours may be approved, with an absolute
 cap of 1,080 hours from opening.

 Scenario 4: Theft report and no accepted receipt, or no current claimant
 The same deadlines and review controls apply. The case records the evidence/acquisition state at opening for audit and
 decision context. Later evidence is considered but does not silently reset the deadline.

 4. Business rules
   ID                Rule

   BR-001            A historical report alone does not block an operation. Current restrictions and active cases govern permissions.

   BR-002            Claim evidence status, acquisition basis, association status, case status, restriction status, transfer status and account
                     status shall remain separate.

   BR-003            Receipt acceptance requires reviewer assessment; it is never automatic.

   BR-004            A claim is eligible for transfer only when its acquisition basis is accepted receipt-backed claim or completed
                     AMANA-approved transfer, and all other checks pass.

   BR-005            A claim without an eligible acquisition basis cannot initiate an AMANA-approved transfer. Later evidence may be submitted
                     against the same claim.

   BR-006            Association release is not a transfer, does not delete the device or history, and does not prove a private sale.

   BR-007            At most one active claimant association may exist per device.

   BR-008            Every new theft investigation has a 720-hour deadline measured from its opening timestamp in UTC.

   BR-009            One extension of at most 360 hours may be approved by a senior reviewer with a recorded reason. The total cap is 1,080
                     hours from opening.

   BR-010            Cases approaching the cap shall be escalated. At the cap, a designated case authority must take ownership and record a
                     decision or a specific next action and review date; no automatic theft finding is allowed.

   BR-011            A provisional restriction must be triaged by an authorized reviewer within 72 hours: confirm continuation, dismiss a clearly
                     baseless report with reason, or escalate. Failure is escalated to the senior queue.

   BR-012            Only one active theft investigation per device is permitted. Additional reports link to it and do not reset the deadline.

   BR-013            A closed case may be reopened only after materially new information is accepted for review; prior decisions remain in
                     history.

   BR-014            Reconsideration reviews an existing decision. Reopening formally reactivates a closed case based on materially new
                     information.

   BR-015            An inconclusive outcome may retain a restriction only with documented necessity, a named accountable reviewer, a review
                     date no more than 30 days away, and a reconsideration route.

   BR-016            A user may report their own device. The user retains case access and communication rights; account deactivation is
                     blocked while they are the affected claimant in an active investigation.

   BR-017            Reporting another person's device alone does not block the reporter's account deactivation.

   BR-018            A pending transfer expires after 72 hours unless completed; the initiator may cancel earlier. Cancellation/expiry releases
                     only that transfer's hold.

   BR-019            An active claim dispute blocks AMANA-approved transfer but permits the current claimant to voluntarily release their
                     association. Independent restrictions remain.

   BR-020            A new conflicting identifier submission is held for review. The existing claimant is not restricted solely because of that
                     submission.

   BR-021            Account deactivation is blocked while the user has an affected active investigation, active claim dispute involving their
                     association, a pending transfer they initiated, or a pending reconsideration request.

   BR-022            If no blocking condition exists, deactivation ends an unrestricted active association and retains its historical record.

   BR-023            After a case closes, a user may deactivate even if a restriction remains. The restricted association is retained but must
                     have a defined resolution path, accountable authority, review date, and appeal/reconsideration route.

   BR-024            If a restricted device has no active claimant, a new user may submit a claim for review; registration does not clear the
                     restriction or permit transfer/release.

   BR-025            A transfer requires explicit recipient acceptance and a final server-side eligibility check.

   BR-026            Public lookup must not reveal whether a specific person has registered or claimed a device, or disclose identities, contact
                     details, private evidence or reviewer notes.

   BR-027            Material claim, association, report, case, restriction, identifier, transfer, reviewer, reconsideration and deactivation changes
                     shall be audit logged.

   BR-028            A reviewer shall not decide a case involving their own device, claim, report, close relationship or declared personal interest.
                     System checks plus reviewer self-declaration are required.

   BR-029            An unresolved identifier-conflict or dispute hold applies to the new submission/disputed transfer as defined in the relevant
                     workflow; it shall not silently freeze an existing claimant.

   BR-030            Public lookup results are informational and are not a legal ownership certificate or guarantee that no theft report exists
                     outside AMANA.

 5.3 Public status precedence
 The effective public status shall use this order: (1) Under review for an active theft investigation; (2) Active theft restriction; (3)
 Restricted pending further review for a retained inconclusive restriction; (4) Restricted pending review for dispute or
 identifier-conflict restrictions; (5) No active theft restriction found. If more than one condition applies, the highest-precedence
 label is returned. No public label may reveal claimant identity or whether a named person has registered the device.

 5.4 Server-side enforcement
 Every protected operation must re-evaluate current account, association, acquisition-basis, transfer, case and restriction state
 on the server at execution time. UI state is not authoritative. State-changing operations must be transactionally protected
 against concurrent requests.

 6. Theft report and investigation workflow
 6.1 Minimum report fields
 Required: authenticated reporter; identifier type and value; allegation category; description; accuracy declaration. Incident
 date/period is required if known and otherwise may be omitted with a reason. Supporting evidence is optional. A receipt is not
 mandatory to report suspected theft.

 6.2 Screening and triage
 Screening checks authentication, required fields, identifier syntax, active case duplication, materially identical reports without
 new information, and rate limits. Missing required fields or invalid identifier syntax returns the submission for correction and
 does not activate a restriction. A duplicate report links to the existing case. A passing report atomically creates or links the
 active case, applies a provisional restriction, and records the audit event. Reviewer triage is due within 72 hours.

 6.3 Deadline and escalation
 • Initial deadline: 720 hours from case opening, UTC.

 • One extension: up to 360 hours, approved by a senior reviewer with reason and audit record.

 • Absolute cap: 1,080 hours from the original opening timestamp; extension does not reset the cap.

 • Warning: 72 hours before the current deadline.

 • At the cap: escalate to senior reviewer and designated project case authority. No automatic outcome.

 If no final decision exists at the cap, the designated authority must accept assignment, record why the case remains
 unresolved, specify the next review action, and set a review date no more than 30 days away. Every continuing restriction must
 have a named accountable authority and review date. This is an exception-controlled continuing review, not an automatic
 finding of theft.

 6.4 Decision criteria
 A finding must be based on the totality of documented evidence, identifier consistency, chronology, source reliability, material
 contradictions, and the claimant/reporter's opportunity to respond. A receipt is relevant but not conclusive by itself. Reviewers
 must distinguish:

 • Substantiated: documented evidence provides a reasonable, recorded basis to conclude the theft allegation is supported
 under the project's approved review standard.

 • Unsubstantiated: evidence does not sufficiently support the allegation.

 • Invalid: the report is malformed, materially false on verifiable facts, outside scope, or otherwise ineligible; record a specific
 reason. An unsubstantiated report is not automatically malicious.

 • Inconclusive: available evidence does not support a reliable determination. Restriction may be retained only under BR-015.

 6.6 Reconsideration and reopening
 An affected user may request reconsideration within 30 calendar days of a decision, stating the decision, grounds and
 available supporting evidence. A senior reviewer who did not make the original decision shall decide within 10 calendar days.
 One reconsideration request per decision is allowed, unless materially new evidence supports a separate reopening request.
 Deactivation is blocked while reconsideration is pending. A deactivated user must have secure limited case access or identity
 recovery to submit the request. Reconsideration reviews the existing decision; reopening reactivates a closed case only after
 materially new information is accepted. Prior decisions and audit events remain.

 6.7 Reviewer assignment and independence
 New cases enter a queue and are assigned to the next available qualified reviewer using round-robin or least-open-case
 allocation. The system must exclude reviewers with system-detectable conflicts (their own user/device/claim/report) and
 require a recorded self-declaration of no other personal interest. A reviewer must recuse if a conflict emerges. Escalations go
 to the senior-reviewer queue; system administrators cannot decide cases solely by virtue of administrator role.


 7. Identifier-conflict workflow
 The system normalizes and validates the submitted identifier and checks for an existing association. A conflict holds the new
 submission, not the existing claimant by default. The existing claimant's transfer, release and deactivation permissions do not
 change solely because a new submission conflicts.

 • Initial deadline: 15 calendar days (360 hours) from opening, UTC.

 • One extension: up to 7 calendar days (168 hours), approved by a senior reviewer with reason.

 • Maximum: 22 calendar days (528 hours). Warning 72 hours before current deadline.

 The reviewer records whether the submission is a duplicate, data-entry error, legitimate additional identifier, or unresolved
 conflict. Confirmed invalid submissions are rejected without changing the existing record. An unresolved conflict at the deadline
 is escalated and remains attached only to the new submission pending an accountable next action and review date no more
 than 30 days away. Device records shall not be merged solely because identifiers look similar. An existing claimant may be
 restricted only by an independent qualifying case or documented authorized decision.


 8. Claim-dispute workflow
 A competing claimant submits the device identifier, acquisition basis, description and available supporting evidence. Screening
 checks required fields, rate limits, active claims and cases. A second active association is never created. A passing request
 creates a dispute case that blocks AMANA-approved transfer but permits voluntary release by the current claimant.

 Voluntary release ends the association dispute because the previous active association no longer needs to be contested. It
 does not prove that the new claimant owns the device and does not remove an independent theft restriction or unresolved
 identifier conflict.

 • Initial deadline: 15 calendar days (360 hours), UTC.

 • One extension: up to 7 calendar days (168 hours), approved by a senior reviewer.

 • Maximum: 22 calendar days (528 hours), with warning 72 hours before deadline.

 If the dispute reaches its cap unresolved, it is escalated to the senior reviewer and designated case authority. The authority
 must record a decision or accountable next action and review date no more than 30 days away. The outcome records the basis
 for ending or retaining an association. A dispute decision does not establish legal ownership.


 9. Evidence and receipt review
 Evidence files are private by default and are accessible only to the submitting user where appropriate, assigned reviewers,
 authorized senior reviewers and technical roles with a documented operational need. Evidence URLs must not be public or
 permanently guessable.

 • Supported formats: PDF, JPEG and PNG only.

 • Maximum file size: 10 MB per file; maximum five files per submission.

 • Validate extension, declared MIME type, file signature, size and safe generated storage name. Store outside the public web
 root or in private object storage.

 • Receipt evidence may be submitted at registration or later against the same claim.

 • Receipt review target: within five calendar days of submission; overdue items escalate to the senior queue. Pending
 evidence is never treated as accepted.

 • Reviewer outcome: accept, reject, or request correction, with a reason and audit event.

 Receipt acceptance changes evidence status through a new review event and does not retroactively authorize transfers or
 remove independent restrictions. A receipt does not conclusively prove legal ownership.


 10. AMANA-approved transfer workflow
 • Source claimant initiates a transfer; server verifies eligible acquisition basis and current permissions.

 • System records a pending transfer with a 72-hour expiry and sends an in-app notification; email is sent when configured.

 • Recipient must explicitly accept within 72 hours. Recipient may decline; source claimant may cancel before completion.

 • On acceptance, the server rechecks the source association, acquisition basis, active restrictions, cases, account state and
 recipient eligibility.

 • Completion changes association/claim state in one database transaction and records the recipient's acquisition basis as
 “Acquired via AMANA-approved transfer.”

 • If declined, cancelled, or expired, source association remains unchanged and only the pending-transfer hold is released.

 • Every initiation, acceptance, decline, cancellation, expiry and completion is audit logged.

 An AMANA-approved transfer is a platform-recorded handover, not a payment service or a legal ownership adjudication. A
 recipient may later resell through AMANA if the current eligibility checks pass.

 12.2 Required relationships and constraints
 • At most one active DeviceAssociation per device, enforced by a database uniqueness/partial-index strategy and transaction
 logic.

 • At most one active InvestigationCase per device, enforced by a database constraint or equivalent serializable locking
 strategy.

 • Identifier uniqueness is enforced after normalization; additional identifiers on one device are allowed only through the defined
 reviewed association flow.

 • A completed transfer must atomically end the source association and establish the recipient association, preserving the
 one-active-association invariant.

 • A transfer completion transaction must lock/recheck the device and all current blocking restrictions before committing.

 • Report screening, case creation/linking, provisional restriction creation and audit event must commit or roll back together.

 • Effective public status is derived from current case/restriction records and precedence; it is not an independently editable
 field.

 • Historical claims, associations, reports, outcomes and transfers are not overwritten to erase prior states.

 • Account deactivation must not cascade-delete records required for cases, security, audit or approved retention.

 • Every restriction must have a source or documented authority, start time, reason, accountable owner and next review date if
 it continues.

 12.3 Transaction and concurrency behavior
 For any operation that could race (transfer completion, association release, case opening, restriction change, new claim), the
 backend must use database transactions and row-level locking or an equivalent isolation strategy. The database remains the
 final guard against duplicate active associations. Failed concurrent operations must return a safe conflict response and create
 an audit event where appropriate.

 16.1 Release-blocking criteria
 Release is blocked if any mandatory acceptance test fails; a protected API bypasses authorization; a transfer completes under
 a blocker; a second active association or active theft case can be committed; public lookup reveals personal registration or
 case details; private evidence is publicly accessible; audit history can be silently altered by an ordinary user; or a continuing
 restriction lacks a source, accountable authority and review date.
